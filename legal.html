What this site does with your data

  • No cookies and no local storage: nothing is stored in your browser.
  • No analytics, no pixels, no advertising, no tracking of any kind.
  • No third-party requests while you read. The three typefaces are served from this domain rather than from a font CDN, so your IP address is not handed to anyone else just for opening a page.
  • One exception, and only when you use it: the contact form is delivered by FormSubmit (formsubmit.co), a form relay that passes your message and your IP address to our mailbox. Nothing is sent there until you press the button, and the form deliberately runs without a CAPTCHA so that no Google script is loaded.
  • No profiling and no automated decisions about you, and we do not use anything you send to train AI models.

If you use the form or email us, we handle what you send: your name, your email address, your company and phone number if you give them, and the content of your message. The form marks the name, email and message as required; company and phone are optional and you can leave them empty. We use all of it to answer you and, if we go on to work together, to deliver that work. The legal basis is your request and the steps needed to take it forward, or our legitimate interest in replying to a business enquiry. It is not used for marketing and there is no newsletter.

We keep it for as long as the enquiry or the engagement needs, plus whatever record-keeping law requires. We do not sell it and we do not pass it to anyone who does not need it to answer you.

Two companies handle data on our behalf, and both are in the United States: GitHub (hosting, which logs visitors' IP addresses) and FormSubmit (the form relay, which sees a submission and your IP address). Using either one means your data is transferred outside the EU. Each publishes data-processing terms for that transfer; ask us and we will send you the link and the safeguard that applies. Nothing is stored on this site itself: there is no database behind it.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of what we hold, have it corrected or erased, restrict or object to how we use it, and ask for it in a portable form. Write to the address above and we will answer within a month. If you believe we have handled your data badly, you can complain to your national data-protection authority; in Spain that is the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos.
