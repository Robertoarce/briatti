How we work

Every engagement follows the same five phases, whether you start with a diagnostic or go straight to a production build. No jargon, no long contracts, no surprises.

  • 5 phases
  • ~12 weeks to production
  • Ongoing optimisation
  1. 1

    Discovery Week 0–1

    We audit your workflows, data landscape and business goals, work out where AI will earn the most, and agree how success will be measured.

  2. 2

    Solution Design Week 1–2

    We architect the system: which models, which data pipelines, which integrations. You get a technical proposal and a project roadmap, and nothing gets built until you approve it.

  3. 3

    Pilot / PoC Week 2–6

    We build a working proof of concept on your real data. That validates feasibility, accuracy and integration before anyone commits to a full build.

  4. 4

    Production Build Week 6–12

    A full engineering sprint: production-grade code, tests, security hardening, deployment pipelines and monitoring. We ship in iterations, with a demo every week.

  5. 5

    Launch & Optimise Ongoing

    You go live with support in place. We watch performance, retrain models, take cost out and iterate on what real usage tells us.

Straight answers

What everyone asks before committing, answered the way we would answer it on the call.

My data is a mess. Is that a problem?

No, that is the normal starting point, and it is what the diagnostic is for. You do not need clean data to begin. You need to know which parts are usable, which are not, and which workflow is worth fixing first.

How long until something is actually working?

The diagnostic is one week. A first working build on your own data usually follows within two to six weeks, and you see it in iterations rather than one delivery at the end.

What do you need from me?

One person who knows where the data lives, and read access to the files or systems. Two hours a week, not a project team and not a steering committee.

Who owns what you build?

You do: the code, the pipeline, the documents, the definitions. It is handed over at the end with a runbook, written so the work does not quietly depend on me being available.

Where does our data live, and who can see it?

The technical proposal states where each part runs, in writing, before anything gets built, including which model provider sees what. Nothing is built until you have approved that.

What if the pilot does not work out?

You owe nothing. The first solution is built at no cost, and if you are not happy with it we stop there. That is the whole point of running it that way.

Will you sign an NDA?

Yes. Send yours and we will sign it before you send anything sensitive.

We already have a data team. Do we still need you?

Then you need less, and probably one specific piece: the models, the evaluation, or the integration. That is a normal outcome of the diagnostic, and a smaller engagement than the full build.
